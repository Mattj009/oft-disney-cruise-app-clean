Making of the Skechers Ad

This one did not spring fully formed from an AI prompt. It started with a pencil sketch, got translated into a first digital attempt, went through a couple of awkward mutations, and eventually became the ad we kept.

← Back to Bonus Vault
Step 1 — The Napkin-Near Concept

✏️The Hand-Drawn Blueprint

The whole thing began with a crude hand sketch: the OFT VIP logo on the left, the couple standing full-length on the right, an arrow pointing toward the “secret,” and the basic product idea underneath.

The joke was already there before any AI got involved: comfortable travel gear, Skechers Slip-Ins, compression socks, and a slogan that had no business being as effective as it was.

“BEND OVER FOR NO ONE!”

Step 2 — Turning the Sketch Into Instructions

📝The Prompt Gets Specific

The rough sketch was then translated into a much more detailed production brief: a full-page magazine ad, split-screen layout, the OFT couple in front of the farmhouse, the farmer in heart-print boxers, knee-high compression socks, and Skechers Slip-Ins.

Even at this stage, the intended structure was clear: OFT lifestyle promises on the left, the visual joke on the right, and the slogan anchoring the bottom.

Step 3 — Version One

🧪The First Real Render

The first polished version had most of the ingredients, but the layout was still searching for itself. The split-page concept was there. The wardrobe gag was there. The product placement was there.

But the hierarchy was muddy, and some of the copy had drifted away from the original idea. It looked like an ad — just not yet the ad.

Step 4 — Version Two

🔧Closer — and a Very Strange Detour

The second version tightened the left-hand copy and improved the balance. It also produced one of the funniest accidental artifacts in the whole process:

“OFFICIAL FOOTWEAR OF ‘BEND OVER FOR NO ONE!’”

That was not the intended relationship between the brand line and the slogan, but it was memorable. The ad was now visibly converging on the final concept, even while still finding new ways to be wrong.

Step 5 — The Final Ad

🏁Everything Lands Where It Belongs

The final version finally sorted out the visual hierarchy: the OFT checklist reads cleanly, the couple dominates the right side, Skechers and Hollow compression socks are grouped logically, “Official Footwear of Old Fart Travel” stands on its own, and the slogan gets the full-width punchline treatment it deserved.

The result still looks intentionally ridiculous — but now it looks professionally ridiculous.

🎬The Actual Story

The fun of this ad is not simply that the finished piece works. It is that you can see the idea becoming itself — from pencil marks, to instructions, to near misses, to one spectacularly misplaced “official footwear” line, and finally to the version that stuck.

That is the making-of feature.
